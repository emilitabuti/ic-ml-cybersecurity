Abstract

CCS Concepts

The stability and adaptability of the intrusion detection systems
(IDS) are of prime importance in the current scenario of continuous combat between cyber attackers and defenders in cyber-crime
activity. Those dedicated rule-based systems have made considerable advancements in detecting established patterns from attacks
but severely lag behind when it comes to detection of emerging
and evolving threats. Thus, this review article attempts to fill this
research gap by synthesizing the recent methodologies and input
from different studies regarding adapting intrusion detection today
using machine learning techniques. The idea is to take into consideration the effectiveness of various ML algorithms along with feature
selection methods and use of ensemble learning techniques for
improving the IDS capability. The proposed methodology includes
important procedures such as data loading and preprocessing, feature selection, exploratory data analysis, training and evaluating the
model, applying a Bayesian Gaussian Mixture Model, performing
threshold comparisons, and analyzing outcomes. Observed results
from training six models, including Random Forest, MLP, LSTM, Logistic Regression, KNN, and Decision Tree, are reported. Among the
six models trained, the Random Forest model emerged the best with
an accuracy of 95.10%, an F1 score of 95.10%, precision of 95.11%,
and recall of 95.10% while accruing a loss of 1.77. The models were
trained and evaluated on the UNSW-NB15 dataset, a well-known
network intrusion detection dataset. For each model, performance
metrics such as accuracy, F1 score, precision, recall, and loss are
reported. In addition, average Receiver Operating Characteristic
Area under Curve analysis, optimal threshold analysis, and performance metric comparisons at various thresholds are included. The
findings highlight the effectiveness of ML methods for adaptive
intrusion detection and the importance of threshold selection for
the optimal detection performance.

Introduction

The battle between attackers and defenses has now marked the
cybersecurity landscape. It demonstrates the need for resilient and
adaptive intrusion detection systems (IDS) [5]. While rule-based
systems can flag every known attack pattern, they do not guarantee
that speeds would be similar for completely new and extremely dynamic attacks [10]. Basically, intrusion detection greatly depends on
its features, which may directly affect machine-learning algorithms
for detecting outlier events [19]. It’s the delicate dance of countless network attributes with the perceptive gaze of ML algorithms
that defines the facility within which a system can differentiate
normal from malicious behavior. This, indeed, just highlights the
importance of feature selection methodologies [2].
Machine learning can be the ray of hope with which we could at
least improve the capabilities of IDS in the context of ever-growing
sophistications in cyber threats [8]. Through the deployment of
self-evolving ML algorithms, one can discover more ordinary yet
untraceable behavioral patterns that could augment cyber security
defense against a wide spectrum of clever attacks [7]. Choice of machine learning algorithms thus determines the redirection planned
for intrusion detection systems and is a factor in overall performance and adaptability [3]. Introduces a novel network security
system combining Artificial Intelligence and Neural Networks for

Literature Review

The literature on intrusion detection systems (IDS) is vast and complex as the evolution of threats and the hazards themselves remain
complex. Historically, rule-based systems have formed the mainstay of IDS-approaches, and have defined attack signatures/rules in
advance as guidelines for spotting already known types of attacks
[5]. While effective in certain scenarios, these systems often try to
keep pace with the quick evolution of cyber threats, highlighting
the necessity for more adaptive and smart methods for intrusion
detection [10].
Machine learning (ML) technologies in the contemporary world
proved to be the most feasible approach toward uplifting the capabilities of IDS such that invisibly delicate patterns consisting of
previously unseen, explicitly malicious behavior could be detected
[19]. In general, intrusion detection tasks would entail enabling the
execution of these algorithms on historical data to adapt an increasingly adaptable cyber defense mechanism for systems within the
obviously changing threat landscape [2]. The primary challenge in
ML-based intrusion detection, however, is relevant feature selection from the vast array of network attributes [8]. Feature selection
methodologies are integral to system execution for IDS by filtering out irrelevant and/or redundant elements and focusing instead
on the most informative ones, which are highly contributive for
distinguishing normal activities from malicious ones [7].
Various ML algorithms serve their own purposes in terms of
target usage in intrusion detection [3]. It combines an extensive
exploration of the uses of artificial intelligence and neural networks
with demonstrating continuous learning and an ambitious outcome
in research [6]. They have demonstrated a systematic approach to
using ML models through extensive comparative tests and in-thereal-world datasets using [14]. It shows an application of Quantum
Particle Swarm Optimization together with LSTM networks, which
yields very high accuracy and low false positive rates [20]. It stresses
Decision Tree to K-Nearest Neighbor algorithms and gains close
to 99% accuracy [18]. The last paper deals more with IoT devices
and implements ML classifiers to feast on 100% detection of attacks
while significantly advocating for pre-processed data [1]. K et al.
utilized logistic regression as a machine learning technique for

network attacks using data mining techniques [9].
From decision trees to support vector machines, there has been a
continuous investigation into varied approaches to achieve superior
detection performance. Consequently, the analysts try to comprehend some of the characteristics of different algorithms that are
appropriate for particular use cases to design intrusion detection
systems that are refined and adaptable. The methodology used to

evolve through experimentation and analysis and hence innovation

Dataset Description

Moustafa et al. [12] created the UNSW-NB15 dataset, which offers a
wide variety of attack types and attributes, using a thorough compilation of network traffic data from UNSW Canberra at ADFA. It is a
useful benchmark for network intrusion detection systems. A range
of file formats, such as reports, PCAP files, BRO files, Argus files,
CSV files, and more, are available for download. The dataset’s raw

ICCA 2024, October 17–18, 2024, Dhaka, Bangladesh

network packets were created using the UNSW Canberra Cyber
Range Lab’s IXIA PerfectStorm application. The goal of this procedure was to create a hybrid dataset that included artificial modern
assault behaviors with real-world routine activities. A total of 100
GB of raw network traffic was captured and saved as PCAP files using the tcpdump utility. The UNSW-NB15 dataset notably includes
nine distinct types of attacks: Worms, Shellcode, Fuzzers, Analysis, Backdoors, DoS, Exploits, Generic, and Reconnaissance attacks.
Twelve methods further enhanced this dataset with technologies
like Argus and Bro-IDS, with which 49 attributes were obtained, all
of which have class tags. The features of these files are comprehensively discussed in the UNSW-NB15_features.csv file. The available
dataset forms four major CSV files, namely UNSW-NB15_1.csv,
UNSW-NB15_2.csv, UNSW-NB15_4.csv, and UNSW-NB15_3.csv,
which host 2,044,000 records. The ground truth data are found in
UNSW-NB15_GT.csv, whereas the record of events is contained
within UNSW-NB15_LIST_EVENTS.csv. A certain percentage of the
data was designated as a training set, UNSW_NB15_training-set.csv,
and a test set, UNSW_NB15_testing-set.csv, to allow training and
evaluation of the model. There are 175,341 records in the training
set and 82,332 records in the testing set, covering both normal operations and different kinds of attacks. The training set accounts
for about 68.08% of this data, whereas the testing set contributes
about 31.92%.
The UNSW-NB15 dataset is an enormous collection of network
traffic information used for intrusion detection system evaluations.
It is composed of 49 features that represent different features of network interactions. Source and destination IP addresses are included
in the collection, along with the port numbers that correspond to
them. It captures the connection status and the transaction protocol that was used, giving an overview of the communication
dynamics inside the network. The entire length of network records,
together with the bytes and packets sent in both directions, is used
to calculate the volume and duration of traffic. The time-to-live
(TTL) values of packets and the number of dropped or retransmitted
packets are recorded. The dataset counts the number of bits per
second that the source and destination loads are consuming and
classifies network services like FTP and HTTP. Mean packet sizes
and HTTP transaction depths are also provided, along with TCP
window advertisement values and sequence numbers. Timing and
jitter metrics are also captured, including TCP round-trip timings
and interpacket arrival delays. The dataset counts many metrics
related to connections, including the number of flows using HTTP
methods or connections that share parameters like address or service. Lastly, it assigns a binary label indicating whether a record is
normal or an attack and categorizes network data into attack types
such as Fuzzers, Analysis, and Backdoor. These characteristics offer an extensive amount of information that may be used to build
machine learning models that efficiently identify and categorize
network intrusions.

Performance Analysis

The performance metrics of various machine learning models that
were applied to the dataset are presented in the bar chart [Figure 3].
Notably, Random Forest was the best performer in terms of accuracy,
with MLP and LSTM coming in close second. This demonstrates the
accuracy with which Random Forest can classify cases. The LSTM
demonstrated remarkable performance by attaining the highest
F1 score, indicating a well-balanced combination of precision and
recall, which is necessary for precise anomaly identification. Additionally, LSTM showed better recall, demonstrating its capacity to
efficiently detect true positives while reducing false negatives. Even
though it showed less accuracy and precision, logistic regression
nevertheless offered insightful analysis of the dataset. On the other
hand, models such as MLP and Decision Tree showed competitive
performance several criteria, indicating that they could find use in
adaptive intrusion detection systems.

7

Conclusion and Future Work

This review states a completely exhaustive synthesis of the current
advancements regarding machine learning techniques in adaptive
intrusion detection systems. Thus, all the study is directed towards
improving the capabilities of the IDS, especially against new and

emerging cyber threats. This evaluation involved several experiments on machine learning algorithms with different feature selection strategies and ensemble learning methodologies. Noteworthy,
rigorous experimentation and analysis of the UNSW-NB15 dataset
showed ML-based techniques, and most especially the Random
Forest model, to have promising performance measures, which
include the accuracy, recall, precision, and F1 score. The way in
which the study on anomaly detection with BGMM and optimization solutions threshold revealed how important it is to carry out
appropriate thresholds for maximum performance in detection.
The analyses performed by using the Receiver Operating Characteristic Area Under Curve demonstrated the efficacy of the data
learned adaptive intrusion detection methods based on machine
learning. Despite the encouraging findings of this study, future
research can explore several directions to improve the ability and
strength of adaptive intrusion detection systems. First off, investigating more complex machine learning algorithms and ensemble
approaches may enhance detection precision and resilience against
cutting-edge cyber-attacks. Methods such as reinforcement learning, meta-learning, and deep learning architecture can be studied
to capture the complex behaviors and patterns associated with both
known and undiscovered threats. The IDS infrastructures may also
support the rapid detection and response to altered cyber threats
via real-time streaming data processing capabilities. Integration
into big data and cloud computing could significantly scale and
make processing large-scale network traffic data more effective.
